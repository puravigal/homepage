How to evaluate POS software for a restaurant or café

The right system should fit your service style, keep checkout practical and help the owner review daily operations.

Restaurant and café operations vary widely. A small takeaway counter may need a different setup from a dine-in restaurant with table service. Before choosing a POS, describe the way orders are taken, prepared, paid for and reviewed at the end of a shift.

Start with your service model

List whether you serve at a counter, use table service, handle takeaway or delivery orders, or combine several methods. Do not assume every POS supports every service model; ask for a demonstration of your actual process.

Review the menu or product setup

Check how the system handles menu items or sellable products, categories, price changes and unavailable items. If you need add-ons, modifiers, combos or recipe-level ingredient deduction, confirm those specific capabilities before committing.

Test billing and payments

  • Can staff find the correct item quickly?
  • Can the workflow handle your payment methods?
  • Can bills be corrected or cancelled with appropriate controls?
  • Are receipts easy to print or share?

Understand inventory needs

Some food businesses track packaged products; others need ingredient-level stock, wastage, recipe costing or supplier purchase workflows. These are different requirements. Make a list of what you need and verify each item in the product, rather than assuming all POS software includes it.

Plan devices, network and staff roles

Test the exact counter device, printer and network conditions. Ask what happens during an internet interruption and how staff access is controlled. Train the team on the common sale, payment correction and end-of-shift review.

Compare the complete cost

Include subscription, devices, setup, support, integrations and any service charges. A demo should show the full workflow that matters to your business, not just a dashboard screenshot.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between restaurant POS and café POS?

The core need is similar, but the workflow may differ. Table service, kitchen tickets, modifiers, takeaway and quick counter service can require different capabilities.

Does every restaurant POS include kitchen display or KOT?

No. Confirm kitchen order tickets, kitchen display, table management and other restaurant-specific features explicitly with the vendor.

What should a small café prioritise?

Prioritise the actual counter workflow, product or menu setup, payment recording, receipt handling, reports, hardware fit and staff usability.
