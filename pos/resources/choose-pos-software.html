How to choose POS software for a small business

Use this practical checklist to compare point-of-sale systems by the work your team actually does—not just by the feature list.

A practical guide from Puravigal

Point-of-sale (POS) software records sales and payments. Depending on the product, it can also connect product information, inventory, purchases, customer records and reports. The right choice is the one that fits your daily workflow and is clear about what it can—and cannot—do.

1. Map your checkout process

Write down what happens from the moment a customer is ready to buy until the bill is complete. Include product search, quantity changes, discounts if you use them, payment methods, invoice sharing and receipt printing. Test this exact flow during a demo or trial.

2. Check product and inventory handling

List the product details your business relies on: names, categories, units, prices, stock quantities and any variants. Ask how purchases and stock adjustments are recorded, how low-stock items are identified, and whether the system fits your product range.

3. Include purchases and suppliers

If you buy stock regularly, the POS should make it possible to record purchases and understand how they affect stock. Check supplier records, purchase history and any balance-tracking requirements that matter to your business.

4. Know which reports you need

Choose the daily questions you want the software to answer: How much did we sell? Which payment methods were used? What stock needs attention? What purchases were recorded? Can the owner find these answers without manually combining multiple spreadsheets?

5. Confirm hardware and connectivity

Before subscribing, check compatibility with your actual phones, tablets, computers, barcode scanners, printers and network conditions. If offline operation is essential, ask the vendor to demonstrate what works without internet, what is stored locally and how synchronisation happens when connectivity returns.

6. Compare the full cost

Look beyond the headline subscription. Include hardware, setup, data migration, staff training, support, integrations and any limits or extra charges. Compare monthly and annual totals on the same basis.

7. Test usability with the people who will use it

Ask the staff member who handles the counter to try common tasks. A system that owners like but staff struggle to use can slow down daily operations. Test corrections and edge cases—not just the easiest sale.

8. Make a short scorecard

  • Billing speed and ease of use
  • Product catalogue and inventory fit
  • Purchase and supplier workflow
  • Reports and exports
  • Hardware compatibility
  • Security, support and data handling
  • Transparent total cost

Frequently asked questions

What is the most important feature in POS software?

Start with a reliable checkout workflow. Then check inventory, purchase records and reporting based on what your business needs every day.

Should a small business choose the cheapest POS?

Price matters, but compare the full cost and the work the product actually saves. A low subscription may not be the best value if essential hardware, support or workflows cost extra.

How can I compare POS software before buying?

Use the same sample products and checkout steps in each demo, then compare the results against your checklist.
